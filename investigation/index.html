Geotechnical site investigation in Brisbane is the systematic process of characterising subsurface conditions to de-risk civil and structural design. It covers desktop studies, intrusive fieldwork, in‑situ testing, and laboratory analysis to define stratigraphy, strength, compressibility, and groundwater regime. The city’s geology—Paleozoic metamorphics, Triassic sedimentary basins, reactive residual clays, and Quaternary alluvium along the Brisbane River—demands investigation strategies aligned with AS 1726‑2017 and local council requirements. A properly scoped investigation is the foundation for safe footings, retention systems, and pavement designs, particularly where highly variable ground or fill is encountered. Early integration of Standard Penetration Testing (SPT) and sampling provides the first reliable indication of density and consistency across these formations.

Fieldwork follows the hierarchy set out in AS 1726‑2017, with methods selected to match the ground model and design data needs. Boreholes advanced by solid‑flight auger or rotary drilling are combined with in‑situ testing to recover continuous strength and stiffness profiles. In cohesive alluvium and soft estuarine clays, the field vane shear test (VST) yields undrained shear strength while minimising sample disturbance. Where granular layers dominate, SPT blow counts and disturbed samples are standard, supplemented by Ménard pressuremeter testing (PMT) to obtain modulus and limit pressure for settlement‑sensitive structures. All work is executed under the supervision of a CPEng‑ or RPEQ‑registered geotechnical engineer, with borehole logs coded to AS 1726, groundwater monitoring installed per minimum 24‑hour stabilisation protocols, and samples transported in accordance with AS 1289 for laboratory classification and strength testing.

Brisbane projects span tight inner‑city basements, major infrastructure corridors, and greenfield residential subdivisions on the outskirts. High‑rise developments in the CBD frequently require deep boreholes into fresh rock to assess socket capacity, while industrial warehouses on the floodplain rely heavily on plate load testing (PLT) to validate allowable bearing pressures beneath shallow footings. Transport projects along the Bruce Highway and Cross River Rail alignment demand rigorous compaction verification, where field density testing (sand cone method) confirms compliance with specification for engineered fill and subgrade. For residential sites with suspected uncontrolled fill or soft spots, exploratory test pits offer a rapid, cost‑effective means of exposing buried services, fill thickness, and root zones before finalising foundation recommendations.

A typical investigation commences with a desktop review of BCC flood overlays, acid sulfate soil mapping, and nearby borehole records, followed by a site‑specific plan that sequences drilling, in‑situ testing, and sampling. Upon completion, the factual report delivers AGS‑formatted logs, laboratory results, and groundwater data. The interpretive report translates these findings into design parameters—bearing capacity, shaft friction, settlement potential, and excavation support categories—directly addressing the requirements of the National Construction Code and local planning schemes. By engaging a specialist team that deploys the right mix of methods from SPT to pressuremeter and vane shear testing, clients receive a defensible ground model that reduces geotechnical risk, shortens approval timeframes, and optimises foundation and earthworks costs across every stage of the project lifecycle.

MASW and VS30 Profiling

Resonant Column & Bender Element Tests

Laboratory dynamic testing on undisturbed tube samples. Measures G0, modulus reduction G/G0, and damping ratio at strain levels from 0.0001% to 1%. Essential for isolation bearing stiffness calibration.

Quick answers

What is the typical VS30 range for Brisbane sites?

Brisbane sites range from Class D ( 180–360 m/s) in deep alluvial fills along the river to Class B (VS30 760+ m/s) on fresh phyllite in the western suburbs. Most inner-city sites fall in Class Ce (360–600 m/s). We recommend a MASW survey for every project to confirm the code classification.

How does base isolation change the geotechnical investigation scope?

The investigation must extend deeper than for a conventional fixed-base building. We typically drill to 30 m or to rock, whichever comes first. Dynamic tests (VS, resonant column) are mandatory. We also measure the natural period of the soil column to check for resonance with the isolation period.

What is the cost range for a complete base isolation geotechnical investigation in Brisbane?

A full investigation including MASW, two boreholes with SPT, resonant column tests, and site response analysis ranges between AU$7.450 and AU$11.720. The final cost depends on the number of boreholes and the depth required to reach stiff stratum.

Can I use a default site class from AS 1170.4 instead of measured VS30?

Yes, but the code default site class is often one class softer than the actual site. This increases the design base shear by 15% to 30% and may force a larger isolation system.
